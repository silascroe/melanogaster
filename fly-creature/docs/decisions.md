# Fly Creature decisions

## 2026-10-09: test a measurable downstream link before another sensory model

The [new contract](pursuit_downstream_contract.md) starts with compatibility of
the public DNa02 and existing AOTU motion-pulse archives, not another retinal
response rule. If state/geometry/coverage can be established, measured AOTU
population templates can test the selected four signed AOTU→DNa02 edges against
equal-strength and altered-route controls. Separate cohorts and omitted inputs
limit this to population compatibility. Unknown metadata stops the experiment;
no useful contact-strength advantage stops the candidate. No new simulator or
Godot integration follows merely from drafting the contract. Phase A now
verifies archive integrity and common coverage, but the
[readiness report](pursuit_downstream_readiness.md) stops on missing stimulus/state
provenance. Preserve unread DNa02 responses; resolve recording/pattern metadata
before a conditional comparison rather than assuming compatibility.

## 2026-10-09: retire the transferred-envelope primary candidate

The [bounded posthoc diagnosis](lc10a_response_diagnosis.md) localizes most
recoverable prediction loss to the borrowed temporal envelope. Source-equation
and native/cache checks pass; the transfer into our activity/AOTU model remains
unsupported. Removing the envelope still fails the joint practical gates.
Preserve frozen results and useful components, but stop tuning this candidate
against the reused archive. Define a new source-constrained question before

## LC10a sensor contract: geometry before a fitted response law

The [contract](lc10a_sensor_contract.md) and [source refresh](lc10a_sensor_source_refresh.md)
separate finite-object geometry, surround, contrast, motion and state from LC10a
response assumptions. The registered map supplies azimuth, not per-cell elevation.
Use the fixed 275 IDs/edges and retain 164 mapped/111 fallback provenance.
The new 68-probe matrix is a synthetic stimulus specification, not a response
experiment. No hard surround-suppression ratio, universal contrast law or complete
regressive silence is supported by the inspected sources.

Final-paper bar dimensions are now available; paper pulse cadence differs from
the predominantly one-second hidden gaps recovered in our archives. Preserve
native timestamps and label any paper-to-recording size association as provisional
until external pattern metadata is recovered. The renderer/runner are now
implemented: synthetic pixels and temporal coverage are inspectable, but output
stops before the first neuron. Choose a response rule only after reviewing this
image boundary and its source constraints.

## Pursuit improvement cycle: reject output correction; define upstream evidence

The [completed cycle](pursuit_goal_result.md) preserves production. A paired
three-reference audit retains negative nonpreferred AOTU025 evoked responses,
but missing long-gap/first-pulse coverage prevents separating target history
from inhibition. The nominal one-second-gap sensitivity is explicitly posthoc;
the original literal cutoff remains reproducible. A frozen three-coefficient
direction-skew/negative-offset correction fails predictive and wiring gates:
P1 error improves 0.058% for 019 and worsens 0.164% for 025. Reject this correction
and stop extending its parameter search. Its recording holds are exploratory
because the design reused the same dataset.

The [replacement plan](pursuit_replacement_plan.md) defines a finite-object LC10a
sensor contract and source-grounded probes before changing the input. Keep graph,
registered-center uncertainty and matched controls explicit. Object-size tuning
does not establish per-neuron receptive-field widths. Do not promote to Godot
without upstream evidence and the existing closed-loop gates.

## Population tuning: diagnose concrete residual features before rewiring

The [matched comparison](pursuit_population_result.md) preserves frozen
anatomy/simple shapes and applies training-only gain fits on identical
recording holdouts. The empirical cell template has additional fitted values;
equal final calibration is not equal complexity. It improves P1 RMSE about
2.2% over anatomy, while anatomy-over-simple benefits remain 0.24%/0.53%.
Anatomy's 019 shape correlation is better in aggregate, but outer-field tails,
025 direction-dependent peaks and below-reference firing remain mismatched.
Do not infer inhibition from baseline subtraction, shift the whole eye map
from coarse pulse-mean peak locations, or credit empirical grand-mean curve
agreement as independent validation. Next audit target history and baseline
choice with recording-blocked evidence; this audit and the subsequent rejected
correction are now recorded above. Production remains unchanged.

## Repeatability: recognize target variation without excusing the adapter

The [completed audit](pursuit_repeatability_result.md) finds repeatable P1
stimulus structure: same-recording templates beat blind means by 14.3%/16.7%
and other-recording templates by 11.0%/10.4%. Simple descriptive movement adds
4.47%/0.85% held-trial RMSE reduction, not a causal motor explanation. NoP1
cell-specific templates lose to blind means. Substantial trial and recording
variation is part of the prediction target, but neither templates nor their
errors establish a biological noise ceiling or validate our sensory adapter.
Keep production unchanged. Next compare frozen anatomy, simple spatial and
empirical other-recording tuning on identical pulse-mean holdouts/coverage
before another sensory rewrite. Do not compare these absolute errors directly
with the earlier full-trace or raw-rate objectives.

## Temporal response: preserve the bounded hypothesis without promotion

The [completed experiment](pursuit_temporal_result.md) replays native whole
trials, excludes the known duplicate and compares equally calibrated temporal
anatomy, simple spatial and 32 shuffled models with nested recording holdouts.
Pooled P1 reductions are 0.81%/0.28%, below the frozen 5% criterion and 60%
recording-win threshold; fast-to-slow transfer worsens both types. Hemisphere
sensitivities do not rescue it. Example-curve improvements do not establish
aggregate or circuit-specific benefit. Keep production and the grid unchanged.
Audit repeated-stimulus response variation and continuous motor covariates
before another sensory rewrite; correlations are not automatically causal
neural inputs. The new evoked time-course objective is distinct from the old
raw pulse-mean calibration, so numerical tables/ranks are not directly comparable.

## P1 physiology: distinguish spatial benefit from circuit-specific benefit

The [P1 extension](pursuit_p1_result.md) adds 47 recordings and 55,252 pulses.
Frozen anatomy reduces error against a constant predictor, but its advantage
against a simple spatial curve is small; footprints add almost nothing.
One numeric recording occurs in both condition archives, and `trackLR` order
is not linked reliably to filenames. Preserve primary results as provisional,
record declared hemisphere sensitivities and post-hoc duplicate exclusion,
and avoid causal P1 or side-specific validation claims. Investigate measured
temporal-response mismatch with a bounded held-recording diagnostic before
retuning production or consuming fresh pursuit paths.

## NoP1 physiology: preserve the empirical baseline, do not promote a kernel

The [completed benchmark](pursuit_physiology_result.md) measures held-out

a fixed simple spatial predictor is close, and an intercept-only predictor
nearly matches quiescent AOTU019 and improves running AOTU019. Wiring ranks
alone therefore overstate practical fit. NoP1 and pulse-window locomotor strata
are explicit conditions, not a calibration of aroused pursuit. The P1
extension is now complete; read its condition and metadata audit before
interpreting the original side assignments. Keep the
original calibration budget, label protocol corrections/post-hoc controls,
and preserve production and fresh behavioral validation.

## Response diagnosis: investigate spatial inputs before a controller fix

The [decomposition](pursuit_response_result.md) locates a large wide-angle gap

regress fast sweeps, so neither is promoted. The source field is image-derived;

## Timing contract: preserve feedback delay and compare matched references

The [final-paper timing audit](pursuit_timing_contract.md) resolves the older

Do not use a delay relabeling as a fix, subtract response latency from the

## First reaction: looming object to jump

The first visible behavior is deliberately narrow: an object approaches a fly standing or walking on the floor, and a DNp01 giant-fiber spike requests a jump animation. Published work identifies LC4 and LPLC2 as direct visual inputs to the giant fiber during looming. The local MaleCNS v1.0 files contain 126 traced LC4, 185 traced LPLC2, and two traced DNp01 neurons; the selected direct path has 311 weighted body-to-body edges. These counts are from the local release, not a claim that this is a complete escape circuit.

## Selection boundary

The exporter includes only traced neurons of those three exact types and only direct LC4/LPLC2 → DNp01 edges. It intentionally omits other partners, upstream retina and optic-lobe circuits, downstream motor circuitry, and connections among or outside the selected nodes. Synapse counts are recorded data. Predicted acetylcholine is an annotation; assigning positive fast current to those edges is a model convention.

## Sensor and motor contract

Godot sends fly position and heading plus the object's position, radius, and active state every 50 ms. Python computes the object's apparent angular size and positive expansion rate when it is inside a 130-degree forward field. LC4 current scales with expansion above 5 degrees/s; LPLC2 current scales with apparent size above 8 degrees while expansion exceeds 3 degrees/s. Those thresholds and the 90 mV maximum external current are engineered, not measured visual physiology. The scene never injects current directly into DNp01.

The neural model is an adapted version of FlyTest's general one-step-delayed LIF runtime: 1 ms steps, 20 ms membrane time constant, -65 mV rest/reset, -50 mV threshold, 2 ms refractory period, and 0.05 mV current scale per recorded synapse. These are simplifying settings. A DNp01 spike requests a jump, gated by a 1.2 s animation cooldown. Walking, object motion, and the ballistic animation are Godot behaviors; they are not neural outputs.

## What the first diagnostic establishes

A fixed 36-frame approach at 50 ms per frame compares identical world inputs and neuron parameters with the recorded direct edges present versus removed. The first local run produced a first intact jump at frame 24 and no disconnected giant-fiber spikes or jump. This establishes signal propagation and action dependence on the included edges in this configured simulation. It does not show biological timing, natural vision, anatomical superiority to a matched alternative, or robust behavior across scenes.

## Portability

The packaged circuit is a compact, attributed derivative of the official release. Runtime source, Godot scene, dependency manifest, and data all live under `fly-creature/`; normal use needs no file from the parent FlyTest lab. The release exporter is development-only and accepts an explicit source directory.

## Visual fly import

The Blender macro-study fly is a presentation asset, not circuit evidence. A
copy of its packed `.blend` lives in `art/`; `tools/export_fly_model.py` turns
it into a self-contained glTF under `godot/assets/`. The export simplifies the
Blender shader graph to image-based glTF materials, converts curves to meshes,
adds a subtle image-based cuticle map to the legs, and keeps wings and
two-segment legs under named pivots. `godot/fly_actor.tscn` combines that GLB
with the reusable gait, jump, and flight pose controller. The current room
instances this actor and supplies position, heading, walking speed, and action requests;
another world can do the same without copying its animation code. The source
artwork and the old arena remain separate. The creature's existing world coordinates, engineered
vision features, Python LIF circuit, and jump trigger do not depend on which
visual mesh is displayed. Godot handles wing twitches, tripod stepping with
grounded foot targets, a two-segment leg solve, takeoff and landing poses,
body bob, and camera follow as presentation behavior. None of these
motions should be described as a simulated fly motor circuit.

The first Blender model had a decorative dark curve intended to suggest a
posterior thorax seam. Rear-angle renders showed it standing away from the
cuticle as a floating arch. We removed that curve from the editable Blender
source and regenerated the Godot GLB. The real thorax shape, wing geometry,
and abdomen bands remain; this is an art correction, not a biological claim.


### Fly model v2 (2026-10-13)

The v1 legs were straight, untapered tubes with a stub tarsus, so the fly
stood on tiptoe and read as a mosquito at close range. `tools/refine_fly_model.py`
rebuilds v2 from the untouched v1 file: real leg segments with approximate
Drosophila proportions (taken by eye, not measured), bristles, claws, pulvilli,
the male sex comb, reshaped antennae and palps, and matte eyes. The
export adds an ankle pivot; Godot solves hip and knee to place the ankle and
holds the tarsus at its rest pose relative to the body, so planted feet lie
flat. Body textures are exported at 75% saturation because the cuticle read as
saturated orange in the game's warm light. The extended proboscis is built in
Godot (`fly_actor.gd`), rooted in the model's proboscis nub and aimed at the
food drop. All of this is presentation, not anatomy data or circuit evidence.
v1 is kept byte-identical in `art/versions/v1-original/`.

The v1 head was a scaled sphere with two eye spheres pushed into its sides
("a ball wearing headphones"). v2 remaps the head mesh point by point onto a
shorter capsule with a flat frons, a flatter back, narrow cheeks and a shallow
facial recess, keeping its UVs and textures; each eye is re-seated with one
affine transform so every facet survives. The antennae sit close together at
the face's midline, the palps and proboscis sit on the head's underside, and
the neck is a narrow dark membrane. v1's clypeus knob is dropped because on the
new head it read as a cartoon nose. Proportions are judged from renders and
reference shape, not measured. Because the proboscis nub moved, `fly_actor.gd`
now derives the extended proboscis's base from the nub's bounds at runtime
rather than a hard-coded point.

The head is a UV sphere wrapped in one square image, so each texel is twice as
wide as it is tall on the surface. The flat v2 face under grazing light showed
that as horizontal wrinkles in the bump. The head's cuticle maps tile left to
right, so v2 repeats them twice around the head, and the exporter halves the
head's horizontal slopes when it turns height into a normal map. Face vertices
are also relaxed along the new surface so the texture spreads evenly.

## Engineered roaming

`G` switches from manual WASD movement to a simple world controller. Its inputs
are the fly's position, heading, and room bounds; it selects nearby random
goals and steers the fly toward them. Its output is desired heading and floor
translation. This controller does not read neural activity or inject current
into the selected circuit. The existing approaching-object sensor and jump
trigger still operate while roaming in the interactive scene. Roaming shows
the model in motion; it is not a connectome behavior experiment.

## Animation recording

`--animation-demo` makes the Godot scene follow a seeded roaming route, take
off, cruise, and land during a ten-second movie. It bypasses the Python
session and hides the neural HUD. This is an asset and animation inspection
clip, not a behavioral experiment; the flight has no connectome trigger.
The interactive scene also exposes flight on `T`, independently of the neural
jump trigger. The world still chooses a horizontal route while the reusable
actor animates a fixed height above its floor position. That is a visual
flight mode, not aerodynamic simulation or a connectome-derived motor command.

## Forward walking gait

The reusable `FlyActor` now uses a modified alternating tripod for authored
walking: the front, opposite middle, and same-side hind legs share a group,
with small front-to-middle-to-hind delays rather than simultaneous swings.
Slower translation extends the world-planted stance interval while the swing
duration remains about 0.12 s. Each recovering foot moves ahead on a low arc;
the body rise is small. These choices follow the qualitative observations in
[real fly walking references](real_fly_walking_references.md), especially Chun
et al. (2021). The numerical timings, phase offsets, and arc heights are
authored for this simplified two-segment rig, not measurements of a real fly.

`--walking-demo` isolates a slow straight walk and stop, without the roaming
controller, flight, neural service, or HUD. The head and body axis were checked
against the Godot forward vector; the earlier clip's turning route and
following camera made the direction of travel hard to read. The demo camera follows
only partway so the animal visibly advances head-first across the floor.
The interactive room still uses the same `FlyActor` gait. Its turning leg
kinematics and foot contacts remain approximate.

## Visual steering candidate and engineered pursuit

The [Male CNS steering inspection](steering_candidate.md) selects traced
LC10a, AOTU019, AOTU025, and DNa02 cells because published object-pursuit and
walking-steering work gives them a concrete sensory-to-output question. The
Male CNS release contains the expected bilateral direct bridges. A signed
two-edge structural score is biased toward the stimulated side and reverses
when the AOTU-to-DNa02 bridge destinations are swapped. This is evidence for
a candidate route under an assumed transmitter-sign rule, not a prediction of
spiking or actual turns. A subsequent [matched LIF probe](steering_candidate.md#matched-neural-activity-probe)
finds a side-specific DNa02 voltage bias with the recorded bridge, zero bias
when that bridge is removed, and a reversed bias when its destinations are
swapped. DNa02 never spikes under the fixed probe settings.

The [pursuit milestone](pursuit_milestone.md) deliberately reads the DNa02
voltage difference as an analog motor command. That readout and its gain are
engineered. Godot moves a visible small target, supplies a left/right bearing
feature to the selected LC10a population, and applies the returned turn rate
while the fly walks forward. Removing only the AOTU-to-DNa02 bridge in the
steering circuit eliminates the turn in the same scripted scene. Three small
closed-loop target paths reduce bearing error relative to that control, but
they do not establish real-fly sensor tuning, neural firing, or pursuit
behavior. The existing jump circuit and roaming rule retain separate roles.

## 2026-09-26: retain the upstream map, reject the simple motion gate

The [upstream and motion report](visual_motion_experiment.md) derives LC10a

axis. Direct upstream input covers 248 of 275 cells, but only 164 have at least

The predeclared promotion gate failed. Keep the packaged map as a research

## 2026-09-26: diagnose sensory drive separately from motor readout

The [paired input/readout audit](input_readout_audit.md) holds a prerecorded
trajectory fixed while comparing upstream-position and speed-sensitive input.
The motion rule roughly halves mean delivered current and suppresses LC10a,
AOTU019, and DNa02 voltage responses. Correct-sign voltage frames become
silent, rather than changing sign. In a separate readout comparison, a
spike-only DNa02 decoder never turns because the model emits no DNa02 spikes.
Thus the extra pursuit loss from the motion rule is primarily a sensory-drive
effect under the existing software readout, while the voltage-based motor
command remains an independent biological-validity limit. Do not change the
playable input or readout on this evidence. Seek a fixed-fly stimulus and
neural parameterization that produce a defensible DNa02 firing response
before claiming a firing-to-steering mechanism.

## 2026-09-26: do not promote tonic firing from the sensitivity grid

The [fixed-fly DNa02 firing experiment](fixed_fly_dn_firing.md) used
literature-matched object-pulse span and speeds, but explicitly modeled
LC10a currents and LIF parameters. Full-strength visual input elicited no
DNa02 spikes at the current settings or with a 2×/4× boost to the AOTU
bridge. Tonic DNa02 drive above the LIF threshold yielded a correctly signed,
bridge-dependent spike difference, but the tonic level is unmeasured and
direction selectivity remained absent. Static targets also produced the
position-input response. Keep both tonic drive and bridge multipliers
experimental; retain the playable voltage rule as an engineered proxy.

## 2026-10-13: grooming and bitter join the whole-brain game; per-sense subsets

Antennal grooming (JO-C/E → aDN1/aDN2) and bitter suppression of feeding (LB1
blocking LB3 → MN9) both passed pre-declared whole-brain tests against controls
([result](whole_brain_result.md)). The combined subset v3 missed the right-turn
gate by 1 point (−21%), so it was not tuned. Instead, each brain copy loads a
subset that passed for its own sense: target keeps v1, and taste, loom and touch
use v3. This costs one extra package in memory and keeps every copy on validated
ground. The bitter descending-neuron screen showed broad activation, so bitter
has no movement reaction; it only prevents feeding. Grooming's dust, threshold,
clearing time and leg sweep are engineered.

## 2026-10-13: courtship song from the visual target, not foreleg taste

The pre-declared foreleg ppk23 taste test failed (pIP10 2.5 Hz) and was not
tuned. Its reported values showed LC10a driving pIP10, which then passed its own
pre-declared test against shuffles and a loom control. The song therefore rides

target sensor makes the fly sing mostly while turning, and it sings to any

## 2026-10-13: an all-core brain engine that must match the old one exactly

The game brain ran at about 0.55× real time on one core. The new parallel engine
was accepted only because it reproduces the one-core engine's spikes and neuron
state bit for bit, so every earlier test result still applies unchanged. Faster
designs that sum spike inputs in a different order were ruled out: they would
change the spikes and require re-running every test. The remaining cost is
memory traffic in spike delivery; further work there was stopped once the game
reached real time.

## 2026-10-13: the female fly is a moving target with a costume

The female is presentation only. She uses the male model with male-only parts
hidden, wanders on a scripted random walk, and enters the brain only as the
existing target position. No female-specific sense (pheromone, shape, size) was
added, so the male would court any target the same way. The stop distance
(1.7 m, about 1.5 body lengths) was chosen for looks after 1 m showed him
climbing on her. It was not chosen to raise the singing rate. His song stays
uneven, because the target encoder drives LC10a only when she is off-centre.
Changing that encoder would mean re-running the steering and song tests, so it
was left alone.

## 2026-10-13: backing up from foreleg touch, with an engineered hold

MDN was given an input by screening every single sensory and lobula columnar
group, then confirming the nominee (foreleg touch) with fresh seeds, shuffles
and a hind-leg control. Foreleg touch was picked over a stronger head-crown
group because the game can plausibly produce it. That pick was made after
seeing the screen, which is why the confirmation used new seeds. It runs in its
own small obstacle copy (subset v4) rather than in a rebuilt v3, so no earlier
gate had to be re-run.

The back-up uses an engineered 0.8 s minimum. As first declared it lasted ~0.3 s
and was barely visible. The hold sets how far the fly reverses; the brain
decides only whether it does. Cut wiring never backs up. An engineered give-up
turn after 1.5 s of pressing keeps a fly with a silent MDN from staying stuck
on the cube.

## 2026-10-13: a public page that runs one brain copy, not a video of the game

The showcase runs the obstacle copy (subset v4) in the visitor's browser rather
than showing recordings, because a live, cuttable brain is the honest version
of the claim. v4 was chosen because it is the smallest copy (4,871 neurons,
1.5 MB) and its reaction has a clean intact-versus-cut contrast. The JavaScript
engine is a port, not a new model: on a Python-recorded input it gives the same
spikes and voltages bit for bit, so only the random input stream differs from
the game. The body is a 2D cartoon with the game's rules (8 Hz threshold,
0.8 s hold, 1.5 s give-up). Its own leave radius (2.0 m) was shrunk after the
first headless run showed the fly could stall against the arena wall. That fix
changed walking only. When a device cannot keep up, the page runs in slow
motion and says so instead of skipping brain time.

## 2026-10-14: a neuron map in the game, drawn at real cell bodies

The game's `N` panel draws every neuron of the three packaged subsets (28,584)
at its cell body from the release, as a 3D point cloud, and colours each by
the sense whose copy fired it. One shared map, rather than one per copy, keeps
the picture of the fly's nervous system whole while staying honest about the
copies: the colour says which copy did the firing. 1,407 sensory neurons have
no recorded cell body (it lies outside the scan), so `tools/export_brain_map.py`
places them at the average position of their partners and the panel says so.
At up to ~14,000 firing neurons per 50 ms step, a per-neuron loop in GDScript
would cost frame time, so the service keeps "steps since each neuron last
fired" with NumPy and sends it as a ready-made 256-wide image that Godot
uploads as a texture; the glow fades on the GPU. The map is display only: no
brain, threshold or action rule reads it, and turning it off (`N`) stops the
service sending it.
