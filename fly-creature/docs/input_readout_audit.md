# Visual input versus motor readout: paired audit

**Decision: keep both experimental changes offline.** The speed-sensitive
visual rule chiefly removes input from this model. Separately, the present
DNa02-to-turn rule uses membrane voltage even though the simulated DNa02 cells
do not spike. The former explains the *additional* pursuit loss from the
motion rule under the fixed decoder; the latter limits what the existing
playable steering result can mean biologically.

## Why these two questions differ

The [previous experiment](visual_motion_experiment.md) changed visual input
and observed worse closed-loop pursuit. Its result alone could not distinguish
weaker sensory drive from a motor readout that mishandles the new activity.
Here, the two visual variants first see **exactly the same 20 ms poses and
target locations**, generated by a direct-bearing controller. This holds
the world trajectory fixed while the packaged 281-cell, 366-edge circuit,
LIF parameters, and voltage decoder stay fixed. We then cross the two inputs
with two readouts in separate closed-loop replays on the same five paths.

The spike-only readout turns at the existing turn cap if one DNa02 side has
more spikes in a 20 ms step. That arbitrary magnitude is immaterial here:
there are no DNa02 spikes to decode. It is a diagnostic counterfactual, **not**
a proposed motor model. The five paths were used in earlier experiments and
are not independent validation.

## Visual input, isolated on matched trajectories

The earlier motion rule multiplies the existing position-dependent current by
`0.2 + 0.8 × min(retinal bearing speed / 25°/s, 1)`. It has no directional
tuning. Across five identical direct-controller trajectories, mean delivered
input strength on in-view frames fell from **0.1963** with position input to
**0.0955** with motion input. Total LC10a spikes fell from **161,231** to
**53,848**; AOTU019 spikes from **855** to **286**. These are simulator
spikes, not measured fly responses.

For frames with the target in view and more than 3° off center, the DNa02
voltage difference pointed toward the target on **359/696** frames with
position input and **120/696** with motion input. It pointed the wrong way on
**zero** frames in either replay. On the remaining **337** and **576** frames,
respectively, the difference was zero at the runtime's reported precision.
The motion rule mainly makes the selected route silent; it does not reverse
its steering sign. AOTU019 activity and the available voltage signal shrink
on every tested path. This paired result explains why restoring the
position-only input improved mean closed-loop error from **17.102°** to
**11.428°** with the same voltage decoder. It does not establish that the
position-only waveform is biologically accurate.

The motion feature also uses *retinal* bearing change. Fly turns contribute to
that change and can cancel target movement. On the five direct-controller
paths, the mean retinal speed was below the mean world-bearing speed on every
path; world-bearing and fly-turn speeds are recorded separately in the JSON.
This is another reason the chosen 25°/s rule is a weak biological assumption.

## Motor readout, held separate

The runtime turns at `0.42 rad/s` per `1 mV` of right-minus-left **mean DNa02
membrane voltage**, capped at `1.2 rad/s`. Those gains are engineered. The
model emitted **zero DNa02 spikes** in either matched replay and in all four
closed-loop conditions. Yet its voltage readout issued nonzero turns on
**527/2000** position-input frames and **467/2000** motion-input frames.
With a spike-only DNa02 readout, both inputs produced **zero turning frames**
and the same **26.849°** mean target-bearing error. The position/voltage and
motion/voltage errors were **11.428°** and **17.102°**.

Experimental studies find that right-left DNa02 *firing-rate* differences
relate to rotational velocity, and that AOTU019/025 activity can drive
steering. They do not establish this project's subthreshold-voltage gain or
LIF coupling. See [Collie et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12212441/)
and the [source review](lc10a_visual_input_research.md). Our voltage readout
is useful as an inspectable software proxy, but its turning cannot yet be
credited to a modeled DNa02 firing command.

## What to do with this

Do not promote the motion factor, and do not replace the playable readout with
the spike-only diagnostic. The next scientific step is a **fixed-fly output
calibration question**: can a documented, defensible visual stimulus and
neural parameter set make the selected MaleCNS route produce DNa02 spikes
with the expected side and timing? Record LC10a, AOTU, and DNa02 activity
against that stimulus before another pursuit gain change. If the answer is
no, report the circuit as a voltage-driven demonstration rather than a
validated firing-to-steering model.
