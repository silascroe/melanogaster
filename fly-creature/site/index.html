The wiring

  • Which neuron connects to which, and how many synapses: FlyEM’s Male CNS v1.0 reconstruction of one male fly.
  • Excitatory or inhibitory, from each neuron’s predicted transmitter.
  • Cell-body positions in the picture (except 333 leg sensory neurons, whose bodies sit in the legs; those are drawn near the foreleg nerve).
Our assumptions

The simplifications

  • Every neuron is the same simple leaky integrate-and-fire unit (Shiu et al. 2024 settings).
  • “Touching” means 150 Hz random input to 288 foreleg bristle neurons.
  • The 8 Hz threshold, the 0.8 s minimum back-up, the turn away and the whole cartoon body.
  • This 4,871-neuron copy was cut out of the 211,577-neuron brain because those neurons were active under foreleg touch.
Checked

The tests

  • Whole brain: foreleg touch drives MDN at 16–17 Hz; zero without input; zero from hind-leg touch; beats 20 of 20 randomly rewired brains.
  • Under the same touch, this copy drives MDN at 14.3 Hz; the whole brain, 13.9 Hz (three runs each).
  • This page’s engine gives the same spikes, bit for bit, as the Python engine on a recorded input.
  • Known limit: held down for over a second, the copy can drift into a state where MDN falls silent.

03The full creature

Godot game, five brain copies

In the gameThe fly also jumps from looming objects, steers toward targets, feeds on sugar, refuses bitter drops and grooms dusty antennae. Each reaction runs on its own brain copy and was tested against shuffled wiring first. Source and records on GitHub.

This is a toy built on real data, not a validated model of fly behaviour. Firing rates, the body and every action rule are simplifications; the records of what was tested live in the repository.
